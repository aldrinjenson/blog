## A small note before I begin

This is a personal reflection, not a recap. I'm deliberately not writing about the sessions, the content, the people, or the specifics of what was discussed — those belong to their owners, not to me. Think of this as a diary entry from a day that meant something, not a newsletter about the day's substance.

## Showing up

I spent March 31st at [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/) in New York, representing [Athena Intelligence](https://athenaintelligence.ai/).

I've been to a lot of student hackathons and conferences over the years. This was my first time walking into a room where I was the youngest person by a noticeable margin, and where nearly every other name card belonged to someone with a title I'd normally only read about in industry write-ups.

I was genuinely nervous.

## The Moleskine moment

I arrived early. Found my name on a table. Sat down. Briefly stared.

*Aldrin Jenson · Athena Intelligence.*

I didn't expect to be affected by something as small as seeing my name on a printed card. But I was. For a few seconds it felt very real — *this is the job, this is the life, you are the person holding this seat today.* I didn't tell anyone. I just breathed and opened the Moleskine.

## Talking about Athena

I got to tell a few folks about what we're building at Athena — our spreadsheet agent in particular, the piece that writes formulas, respects formatting, and produces multiple document types from a single input. I didn't do a live demo — I just talked, sketched the shape of it, and watched people nod.

The thing I kept coming back to in every conversation is that enterprises don't actually want *more* AI tooling. They want **fewer** surfaces that do *more*. "Tell the thing what you need in one place; let it produce the five artifacts downstream." That frame seemed to land.

## Going solo

The most unexpected thing wasn't the content — it was the feeling. Walking in alone as the only Athena person in the room. Realizing for one full day that "the company" in the room was me.

A few times, someone asked me a question I couldn't answer. I said, *"I don't know — let me find out and get back to you."* Which, it turns out, is what everyone in a room like that also says, just with more gray hair.

## Afterthoughts

A few things I was sitting with on the train back:

* **Showing up is most of it.** I nearly let imposter anxiety talk me out of being fully present. I'm glad I didn't. You learn more in one day of being in a room than in six months of reading about what happens in that room.
* **My job is also a privilege.** Not every early-career engineer gets to represent a company at a place like this. I want to remember that instead of only the stress of it.
* **Gratitude to IBM** for the generous welcome — and to my team for the trust.

That's the post. No hot takes, no "ten things I learned." Just a good day in New York that I wanted to remember.
